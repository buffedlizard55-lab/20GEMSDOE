# Ranked geological hypotheses — H21 register (2026-10-01)

**Status labels:** OBSERVED = official/trusted source statement; COMPUTED = result from a named local artifact; INFERENCE = a testable geological/modeling idea. No candidate has demonstrated improvement on hidden-fault leaderboard DTI. Expected-gain ranges for untested ideas are **subjective planning priors**, not measured estimates, confidence intervals or forecasts. H21-1 has now been tested on a known-catalogue transfer proxy; its measured proxy result is reported separately and replaces its former planning prior.

## Rules that constrain the hypotheses

1. DrivenData staff defines a new fault as a pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS; it may be an extension/splay of a known system. [Staff definition](https://community.drivendata.org/t/where-do-you-draw-the-line/11536/2)
2. The known-fault exclusion is pixel-exact. A prediction near a known trace is not automatically exempt from FP penalty, and a new fault can lie within 300 m of a known trace. [Staff clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4)
3. The organizers do not disclose the hidden labels' source/fault type. Geological plausibility is not evidence of leaderboard utility.
4. The stored H20 holdout results are not reproduced, use known-catalogue labels and a synthetic sparse proxy; H20 calibration labels are synthetic; the stored spatial slice was used for two candidates. See [F17 and F25–F29](../audit.html#F17).
5. A candidate slot requires a better result than the reproducible current holdout best under the predeclared comparison rule. Passing a known-catalogue proxy remains necessary evidence only, not hidden-fault validation or automatic authorization.

## H21-1 preregistered result and decision

The single frozen run used the preregistered transform and input hashes recorded in [`evidence/h21_seismic_ridge_holdout.json`](../../evidence/h21_seismic_ridge_holdout.json). It returned Dense `0.06207` and Sparse `0.02511` over four known-catalogue spatial folds. The raw scalar control scored Dense `0.07977` and Sparse `0.02849`. Both the historical H20 comparison and the newly reproduced H16-1 comparison failed every gate rule; H21-1 lost all four folds on both tracks and had a worst per-fold Dense delta of `−0.18618` and Sparse delta of `−0.07084` versus reproduced H16-1.

This is a negative known-catalogue transfer result, not proof that seismicity is useless for hidden-fault detection. It is also not a basis to tune the scales/threshold after the fact: the preregistered test was run once with no sweep. H21-1 is rejected as a submission candidate. The next proposed idea (H21-2) requires its own data/schema audit and frozen spatial test before any recommendation; no slot is authorized.

The H16-1 reference is reproduced separately at [`evidence/spatial_holdout_h16_results.json`](../../evidence/spatial_holdout_h16_results.json): Dense `0.21269`, Sparse `0.08554`, with folds recorded there. H20's stored thresholds are retained as conservative historical comparators but remain unreproduced. These are all known-catalogue proxies; none is hidden-fault ground truth.
