# Ranked geological hypotheses — H22 register (2026-10-01)

**Status labels:** OBSERVED = official/trusted source statement; COMPUTED = result from a named local artifact; INFERENCE = a testable geological/modeling idea. No candidate has demonstrated improvement on hidden-fault leaderboard DTI. Expected-gain ranges for untested ideas are **subjective planning priors**, not measured estimates, confidence intervals or forecasts. H21-1 and H21-2 have each completed exactly one preregistered known-catalogue transfer test; their measured proxy results are reported separately below and replace their former planning priors.

## Rules that constrain the hypotheses

1. DrivenData staff defines a new fault as a pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS; it may be an extension/splay of a known system. [Staff definition](https://community.drivendata.org/t/where-do-you-draw-the-line/11536/2)
2. The known-fault exclusion is pixel-exact. A prediction near a known trace is not automatically exempt from FP penalty, and a new fault can lie within 300 m of a known trace. [Staff clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4)
3. The organizers do not disclose the hidden labels' source/fault type. Geological plausibility is not evidence of leaderboard utility.
4. The stored H20 holdout results are not reproduced, use known-catalogue labels and a synthetic sparse proxy; H20 calibration labels are synthetic; the stored spatial slice was used for two candidates. See [F17 and F25–F29](../audit.html#F17).
5. A candidate slot requires a better result than the reproducible current holdout best (H16-1: Dense `0.21269`, Sparse `0.08554`) under the predeclared comparison rule. Passing a known-catalogue proxy remains necessary evidence only, not hidden-fault validation or automatic authorization.

**Cross-test observation (INFERENCE, hypothesis-generating only):** both preregistered single-mechanism tests (seismic lineations; chemistry-coherence points) lost the known-catalogue proxy to the H16-1 multi-feature ridge synthesis by wide margins, and the C3 control shows plain catalogue proximity yields only ~`0.042` mean Dense on this protocol. This weakens the family of "single sparse point/line co-occurrence" hypotheses and strengthens the case for multi-feature composed surfaces; it is not proof about hidden-fault utility.
