## Scientific hypothesis, novelty and obtainability

**INFERENCE:** if the same textured lithologic marker appears on either side of a fault but with a lateral shift, magnetic and radiometric profiles may match better at a consistent nonzero along-strike lag than with no shift. This is *kinematic matching*, unlike H16's gradient/edge amplitudes, strike worms or alteration product. No inspected prior implementation estimates such a paired displacement. This does not establish novelty across all literature or uninspected repositories.

**OBSERVED physical support:** [USGS fault FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-a-fault-and-what-are-different-types) explains relative block motion and normal/strike-slip/oblique slip; normal faults are common in the Basin and Range. It does not validate this detector. This prototype will preferentially detect recognizable lateral displacement and may miss the predominant normal-fault geometry. No expected positive DTI effect is treated as established.

**Actual free inputs available:** competition RTP band 2; official [GeoDAWN magnetic/radiometric survey](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ, CC0, resampled/quantized radiometric K stack inherited from the hash-verified pinned 7GEMSDOE extraction. Core/external restored bytes are hash-verified, not newly refetched official raster downloads. The 100 m grid does not imply 100 m independent survey resolution (400 m primary flight-line spacing). Read `geodawn_rad.json` provenance; nodata is encoded 0. Quantization lacks retained physical per-channel limits, so K is a **texture observation**, not a physical concentration or K/Th ratio.
