Why consider positive-unlabeled learning?

Inference: an incomplete catalogue makes it plausible that pixels outside known traces include both background and unlabelled faults. That motivates testing PU methods. Kiryo et al.'s nnPU estimator addresses PU risk under mixture assumptions and a specified or estimated class prior; the method does not validate those assumptions for this dataset.

The frequently cited π = 0.0363 is a power-law extrapolation below a selected trace-completeness cutoff, not an observed hidden-pixel prevalence. Treat it as a prior scenario. The local implementation is in src/gems/pu_learning.py; see Kiryo et al. (2017).

Calibration report status

The previous report constructs s_hit from distance to known catalogue/SGMC features, computes a selected-prior-based p_hidden_u, then samples y_pu_hidden_truth = s_hit OR Bernoulli(p_hidden_u). Its Brier score, Murphy REL/ECE and “0.70-bin hit rate” therefore measure agreement with a synthetic proxy target. They do not verify calibrated probabilities on recovered or observed hidden faults. We withdraw the phrase “Brier-verified.” No independent hidden-fault reliability diagram is available.

H21-1 preregistered test

The frozen H21-1 transform uses multi-scale Hessian line ridges from band 16, ieq_n100a15, and compares against the scalar seismic-intensity feature at the same fixed budget. Its protocol is in research/preregistration_h21.md. USGS Great Basin studies motivate a test of seismicity/structure relationships but do not show that this particular layer or transform improves contest DTI.

H21-1 proxy result: Dense 0.06207, Sparse 0.02511; raw scalar control Dense 0.07977, Sparse 0.02849. It did not pass both comparator screens. The H20 comparator remains historical and unreproduced; H16-1 is the current reproduced known-catalogue baseline. Even passing both would be only a necessary proxy screen, not hidden-fault validation or automatic upload authorization. Full report: evidence/h21_seismic_ridge_holdout.json.

Rules that constrain the hypotheses

  1. DrivenData staff defines a new fault as a pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS; it may be an extension/splay of a known system. Staff definition
  2. The known-fault exclusion is pixel-exact. A prediction near a known trace is not automatically exempt from FP penalty, and a new fault can lie within 300 m of a known trace. Staff clarification
  3. The organizers do not disclose the hidden labels' source/fault type. Geological plausibility is not evidence of leaderboard utility.
  4. The stored H20 holdout results are not reproduced, use known-catalogue labels and a synthetic sparse proxy; H20 calibration labels are synthetic; the stored spatial slice was used for two candidates. See F17 and F25–F29.
  5. A candidate slot requires a better result than the reproducible current holdout best under the predeclared comparison rule. Passing a known-catalogue proxy remains necessary evidence only, not hidden-fault validation or automatic authorization.

H21-1 preregistered result and decision

The single frozen run used the preregistered transform and input hashes recorded in evidence/h21_seismic_ridge_holdout.json. It returned Dense 0.06207 and Sparse 0.02511 over four known-catalogue spatial folds. The raw scalar control scored Dense 0.07977 and Sparse 0.02849. Both the historical H20 comparison and the newly reproduced H16-1 comparison failed every gate rule; H21-1 lost all four folds on both tracks and had a worst per-fold Dense delta of −0.18618 and Sparse delta of −0.07084 versus reproduced H16-1.

This is a negative known-catalogue transfer result, not proof that seismicity is useless for hidden-fault detection. It is also not a basis to tune the scales/threshold after the fact: the preregistered test was run once with no sweep. H21-1 is rejected as a submission candidate. The next proposed idea (H21-2) requires its own data/schema audit and frozen spatial test before any recommendation; no slot is authorized.

The H16-1 reference is reproduced separately at evidence/spatial_holdout_h16_results.json: Dense 0.21269, Sparse 0.08554, with folds recorded there. H20's stored thresholds are retained as conservative historical comparators but remain unreproduced. These are all known-catalogue proxies; none is hidden-fault ground truth.
